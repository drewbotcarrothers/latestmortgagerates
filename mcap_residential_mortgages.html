Payment frequency

You can choose the mortgage payment frequency that works best for you:

Monthly:

When you make a mortgage payment on the same day every month. You make 12 payments in a year.

Semi-Monthly:

When your mortgage payment is divided equally between two dates of the month, such as the 1st and 16th. You make 24 payments in a year.

Accelerated Bi-Weekly:

When your monthly mortgage payment is divided by two and you make a mortgage payment every second week. This is considered an 'accelerated' payment schedule because you make 26 payments – the equivalent of one extra monthly payment every year.

Accelerated Weekly:

When your monthly mortgage payment is divided by four and you make a mortgage payment every week. This is considered an 'accelerated' payment schedule because your weekly payments are slightly more than a weekly mortgage payment.

Weekly:

When your monthly mortgage payment is multiplied by 12 months and divided by the 52 weeks in a year. You make 52 payments per year.

Current prime rate

The prime rate is the annual interest rate that major banks and lenders within Canada use to set variable rate mortgages, such as the MCAP adjustable rate mortgage. The prime rate is influenced by the rate set by the Bank of Canada.

Residential Mortgage FAQ

Mortgage specialists are employed by a lending institution, such as a mortgage finance company or bank, to sell that lender’s mortgage products.

Mortgage brokers belong to a brokerage and have access to multiple lenders. With access to more mortgage products, brokers can help you find the best mortgage for your needs. They also require ongoing accreditation and education to maintain their licenses.
A good mortgage broker will have your best interests in mind and take the time to explain the details of your available mortgages. Visit the Find a Broker tool to find a local mortgage broker.
With a fixed rate mortgage, your interest rate is locked in for the full term. That means that principal and interest payments will remain the same throughout the term of your home mortgage.

An adjustable rate mortgage is tied to interest rates. If interest rates fluctuate up or down, your mortgage rate will do the same. The benefit is that if rates decrease, payments do as well. With an adjustable rate mortgage, you can convert to a fixed-rate mortgage anytime, so you can easily take advantage of falling rates.

Both mortgage types allow you to pay down your mortgage faster with lump-sum and prepayment privileges.

When choosing a mortgage, it’s also vital to consider the flexibility of the payment schedule, the options available to pay down the mortgage faster, and whether you can take your residential mortgage with you when you move.
